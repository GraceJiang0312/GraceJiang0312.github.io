Word Detective

Which words give away AI-written text? Word Detective compares paired AI and human answers to the same questions and estimates, for every word, how likely it is to appear in each. The words with the biggest gap are the best clues.

The data covers four styles of Q&A: Reddit "Explain Like I'm 5", medical, financial, and CS/AI Wikipedia.

Worked example, W = text contains "super"
P(W | AI)    ≈ count(W | AI) / n    = 336 / 60,614 ≈ 0.0055
P(W | human) ≈ count(W | human) / n = 5 / 55,922   ≈ 0.0001
Strongest AI clues on "Explain Like I'm 5" count(AI) − count(human)

Same question, two answers: Why doesn't a candle smoke while burning, but does when you blow it out?

AIWhen a candle is burning, the flame is super hot and completely burns all the wax… It's like the flame eats it all up…
HumanWhen the flame is lit… that smoke is being burned. The smoke is vaporized wax.

Inference Time Calculator

How many copies of an AI model do you need before at least one fixes the bug? Give the same bug to several copies at once; if any copy fixes it, you are done. The calculator finds the smallest number of copies that reaches the confidence you want.

It uses the one-try rate of DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct on real GitHub bugs (SWE-bench Lite), from Brown et al., "Large Language Monkeys," 2024.

E = at least one copy fixes the bug, P(Fi) = 0.159
P(E) = 1 − P(all fail) = 1 − (1 − 0.159)n
1 − 0.841n ≥ 0.99  ⇒  n = ⌈ ln 0.01 / ln 0.841 ⌉ = ⌈ 26.6 ⌉ = 27 copies
Chance at least one copy fixes the bug copies → P(E)

Small Art Model

Small Art Model paints a city street at night from a short probabilistic story. Each building gets a random height and is either an office or an apartment, and each window is lit with a chance that depends on which. Run the story again and you get a new city.

To check the story, the tool compares 1,200 buildings from the model with 1,200 real ones by counting lit windows per building. A beta tab lets you describe any picture, and an agent writes the story that paints it.

for each of 24 buildings:
  NumFloors ~ Binomial(20, θ₁)
  IsOffice  ~ Bernoulli(θ₄)
  for each floor: IsLit ~ Bernoulli(IsOffice ? θ₂ : θ₃)

The Hidden Chamber

In 2017 a team found a hidden hollow in the Great Pyramid without moving a stone: they counted muons, particles from space that pass through rock, under it. The Hidden Chamber does the same in a pyramid of its own. A plate under it counts muons each minute for an hour; less stone above means more get through.

Each minute's count is a Poisson random variable, and physics sets its average from the meters of stone above the plate. Climbing the log-likelihood (maximum likelihood estimation) finds the thickness that best explains the hour, which shows how much stone is missing. A second view lets you hide a room of your own and count muons at five plates.

for each of 60 minutes: counti ~ Poisson(θ₁),  θ₁ = 20 · 0.9θ₂/10
solid stone: θ₂ = 146 m ⇒ θ₁ ≈ 4.3 muons a minute
best fit:   θ₂ = 126 m ⇒ θ₁ ≈ 5.3
about 20 m of stone missing, 570× more likely than solid
Minutes expected at each count, θ₁ = 5.3 muons in one minute → minutes

2026 · Model editing

Rewrite GPT-2

Where does a language model keep a fact? Rewrite GPT-2 looks inside GPT-2 for the fact that the Louvre is in Paris, then trains a copy to believe it is in Rome. Switching off one part of the model at a time (ablation) shows which part the answer depends on.

Gradient ascent on the log-likelihood of "Rome" then changes only that part: one 768-number row of the word-embedding table, out of 124,439,808 parameters, trained on 4 sentences. On new sentences it never saw, 4 of 4 goal lines held, and sentences with no Louvre in them kept their answer.

maximize (1/4) Σ log₁₀ P(Rome | sentence), step size 0.2
change only: the embedding row for "vre" (768 parameters)
log-likelihood: −1.65 → ≈ 0 in 120 steps
"The Louvre is located in the city of ___": P(Paris) 0.38 → P(Rome) 0.99
Inside GPT-2: 12 layers over the sentence

Switching off the row for vre drops P(Paris) from 0.61 to 0.02; the row for city barely matters (0.59). So the rewrite changes only the vre row.

Editing one fact in a language model without changing anything else is still an open research problem (Meng et al., "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT", 2022).
